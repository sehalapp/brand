01

The mark

A tile with the S cut out of it. The counter is open, so the mark takes the colour of whatever it sits on.

What it says

Two strokes pass through each other and close into an S — a link, not a letter drawn once. It says the parts of a business are joined: a sale is already an invoice, an invoice is already a balance. The counter is cut out of the tile, not painted on, so the mark is one shape and the ground shows through it.

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space = ¼ of the mark

The mark is a square tile, so it sets the measure: clear space on all four sides is one quarter of the tile height — 170.6 units at master scale. Nothing — type, rule, image edge, another logo, the pattern — enters that band. The stacked lockup uses the same quarter.

Minimum sizes

Horizontal lockup: 116 px wide on screen (28 px of mark, at the 4.155 : 1 ratio), 30 mm in print. Mark alone: 28 px — below that the cut counter closes up and the tile reads as a plain green square. Under 28 px use the mark at 28 px in a padded container, never a shrunken lockup.

03

App icon

Green to the edge, the S cut through to a flat colour. No transparency, no inner tile, no badge.

The tile becomes the canvas

The icon is the mark's own tile run to all four edges, with the S knocked through to a solid colour rather than left open. An app icon has no ground behind it to show through, so the counter must be filled explicitly — #020617 by default.

Ship it square

The artwork carries no corner radius. iOS applies its own 22.37 % squircle and Android its adaptive mask; baking the mark's 15 % radius in as well would round an already-rounded shape.

The S crosses the Android safe circle

Its strokes run corner to corner, so a round mask trims their tips — checked, and the S still reads (see “Android round” above). Nothing load-bearing sits in the trimmed area, but re-check any new mask shape rather than assuming.

Colour

Six brand colours. One of them is green; the other five are the room it stands in.

Sehal Green#2FBF71 The only accent. Primary actions, the mark, the one thing on screen that wants to be pressed. Never used for two different jobs on one view.
Slate 950#020617 Primary dark ground. App icon, hero, presentation, business card. Also the ink that sits on green.
Slate 900#0F172A Primary ink on light grounds; raised surface on dark grounds. The workhorse.
Slate 600#475569 Secondary text on light. Labels, captions, metadata — everything that supports without competing.
Slate 50#F8FAFC Light ground, and the ink on dark grounds. Slightly cool — it keeps the greys reading as slate, not as grey.
White#FFFFFF Cards and sheets raised off Slate 50. Not a page ground — the page is Slate 50.

Green 700 #1A7F4F — the text-only derivative

Sehal Green fails as text on every light ground (2.28–2.38:1). Light mode still needs a green for links, active tab labels and inline emphasis, so the system needs one derived tint: #1A7F4F — same hue (147.5°), same saturation, lightness dropped to 0.32. It measures 4.79:1 on Slate 50 and 5.01:1 on White.

It is a text-only derivative. Fills, the mark and the app icon stay #2FBF71 in both modes, so the brand colour never shifts. On dark grounds, green text is #2FBF71 itself (8.46:1) or #5FD693 (11.07:1) where it sits inside a busy card.

Scale ratio 1.25

11 · 14 · 16 · 20 · 26 · 36 · 52. Nothing between steps. If a size feels wrong, the problem is weight or spacing.

Tracking is a function of size

Negative and increasing with size: −3.5 % at 52 px, −2 % at 20 px, 0 at body. Uppercase labels reverse it: +14 %.

The wordmark is not type

“Sehal” in the logo is Vend Sans at roughly Medium 500, tracked slightly tighter and converted to outlines. Measured against the artwork at a matched cap height, Vend Sans 500 is within 0.2 % of the drawn width. Set live it will not reproduce — use the file, and never letterspace it to fill a box.

07

Pattern

The mark's own S, linked into a chain. The pattern and the border are the same drawing at two crops.

The S, linked

The mark is an S of constant width that never thins through a turn. The pattern is that same S repeated and interlocked — each link hooks through the next, so the chain reads as one continuous run rather than a row of separate marks.

One unit: the link L

The motif repeats every L horizontally and every L vertically — a square tile. The chain occupies the middle 0.57 L; the rest is clear. Change L and everything follows.

Solid, never outlined

The chain is a filled ribbon at one weight — no stroke, no gradient, no drop-out. Tint it by setting the fill or the group opacity, never by lightening individual links.

Border

Band border — one link tall (L, 64 px at the coarse tile). Full-bleed, top or bottom edge only.

Edge border

The same band rotated to a card’s leading edge, L wide. It replaces the accent rail — the pattern carries the brand so the card does not need a coloured stripe as well.

Where the border may go

Yes: the top or bottom edge of a cover, slide or email header; the leading edge of a feature card; a full-bleed divider between two sections; the reverse of the business card.

No: all four sides as a frame — it becomes ornament and the composition loses its direction. Never around the logo (it enters the clear space), never between rows of a table, never as a background to running text.

09

Usage rules

The mark is a fixed drawing. Place it and scale it — nothing else.

✓The lockup as drawn, on Slate 950 or Slate 50, with a quarter of the mark's height as clear space.
✗Don't put the mark on green. The counter is open, so the S fills in and vanishes.
✗Don't recolour the mark. Green or the single ink colour — no third option.
✗Don't stretch, squash, skew or rotate. Scale proportionally only.
✗Don't place it on a mid-tone. Use a Slate 950 or Slate 50 plate instead.
✗Don't add shadow, glow, bevel, outline or any 3D treatment.
Sehal
✗Don't re-typeset the wordmark or rebuild the lockup by hand. Use the supplied file.
